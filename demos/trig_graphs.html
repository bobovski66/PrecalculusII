Graphs of the Six Trigonometric Functions

Modules 2–3 · Periods, asymptotes, amplitude, and transformations

Choose a trig function, then explore its graph on Cartesian axes. Move the observation angle to connect a point on the graph with its trigonometric value.

Blue curve: selected function · dashed orange: vertical asymptotes · green: midline · orange point: current angle (when defined). Curves stop at asymptotes rather than joining across them.

Investigate the six graphs

  1. Compare sine with cosine. How do their shapes relate? How do their values at 0 differ?
  2. Compare the periods of sine and tangent. What changes when B increases?
  3. Graph tangent and cotangent. Locate their vertical asymptotes and explain where they come from using sine and cosine.
  4. Graph secant and cosecant. Why are there disconnected branches, and how do their graphs relate to cosine and sine?
  5. Set A to a negative number. How does reflection change the graph? Is the result bounded?
  6. Change C and D. Distinguish moving an asymptote from moving a midline.

Remember: the independent variable is an angle. One complete revolution is 360° or 2π radians, even though the graph is drawn on ordinary Cartesian axes.
