Module 9 Applications

From repeating steps to predictions

1. An auditorium grows row by row

A small auditorium has 14 seats in its first row, with two additional seats in each succeeding row. There are 20 rows, and the seating plan follows this rule exactly.

  1. Write an explicit formula for the number of seats in row n.
  2. Determine how many seats are in the last row.
  3. Find the total capacity of the auditorium using the arithmetic-series formula.
  4. Explain why multiplying the first-row count by 20 would be incorrect.

2. The cost of an improving battery

A battery pack initially costs $800. Manufacturing improvements reduce its price by 8% per year. Suppose this percentage reduction remains constant.

  1. Write an explicit formula for the price after n years, taking n=0 to mean today.
  2. Predict the price after five years.
  3. Explain why this is geometric decay and not subtraction of $64 each year.
  4. What assumption in this model could become unrealistic over a long period?

3. A bouncing ball

A ball is dropped from a height of 4 meters and rebounds to 60% of its previous maximum height after each impact. Ignore air resistance and assume every rebound follows this same ratio.

  1. Write the first five maximum rebound heights.
  2. Find the sum of the first five rebound heights.
  3. Compute the limiting total distance traveled, including the initial drop and both upward and downward travel for every rebound.
  4. Explain why the finite total is possible even though the mathematical model includes infinitely many bounces.

4. A feedback-controlled adjustment

A controller adjusts a temperature estimate by replacing it with the average of the current estimate and a target of 20°C. Begin at an estimate of 4°C and write a₁=4, aₙ₊₁=(aₙ+20)/2.

  1. Calculate the next four estimates.
  2. Show that the error eₙ=aₙ−20 is multiplied by 1/2 at every step.
  3. Find an explicit expression for aₙ and use it to calculate a₁₀.
  4. Explain why a sequence need not itself be geometric even if its distance from an equilibrium is geometric.

Modeling reminder: pay attention to whether the index begins at 0 or 1. An index convention changes the exponent in an otherwise identical model.
