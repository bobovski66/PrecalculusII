Module 7 Applications

Complex numbers as geometric operations

1. Navigating by distance and bearing

A field rover travels 8 km from its starting position along a direction 120° counterclockwise from east. The rover's software records its position in east and north components, while the navigator reports its direction as a distance and angle.

  1. Write the displacement in polar form and convert it to Cartesian coordinates.
  2. Explain why the east component is negative.
  3. Give another polar representation using a negative radius and verify that it describes the same point.

2. Rotating an image in the plane

A point on an image has complex coordinate z=3+2i. A software transformation multiplies every pixel coordinate by i, which has modulus 1 and argument 90°.

  1. Calculate iz in rectangular form.
  2. Describe the geometric motion applied to the point.
  3. Calculate i²z and i⁴z. Explain why four successive quarter-turns restore the original point.

3. Combining two transformations

An animation engine scales a shape by 2 and rotates it 30°, then applies another transformation that scales by 1/2 and rotates 75°. We can represent the two operations by complex factors.

  1. Write each factor in polar complex form.
  2. Determine the net scale and total rotation without multiplying rectangular expressions.
  3. Explain why multiplying the factors in either order gives the same result for these two-dimensional scaling-and-rotation operations.

4. Roots form a pattern

Suppose a mathematical model asks for all complex numbers z satisfying z⁴=1. Rather than trying random values, use polar form and the periodicity of angles.

  1. Find the four distinct fourth roots of 1.
  2. Plot or describe them on the unit circle.
  3. Explain why their arguments differ by 90° and why they form the vertices of a square.

Complex multiplication is not merely arithmetic with i. It is a compact language for combined rotations and changes of scale.
