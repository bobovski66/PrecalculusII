Module 3 Applications

Periodic functions in motion, sound, and measurement

Related interactive demo: Sinusoid Explorer. Open the demo to explore the mathematical ideas while working through these applications.

1. Ferris wheel: interpreting a periodic model

A passenger boards a Ferris wheel with radius 12 m. The center of the wheel is 14 m above ground, and the wheel makes one counterclockwise revolution every 48 s. The passenger begins at the rightmost point of the wheel, level with its center.

The height after t seconds is h(t)=14+12 sin((π/24)t).

  1. Explain what the numbers 14, 12, and π/24 mean, including units.
  2. At what time does the rider first reach the top? Find the maximum and minimum heights.
  3. Determine the height at t=8 and t=36 seconds.
  4. Suppose the passenger begins instead at the lowest point. Write a cosine model that reflects the new starting position.

2. A vibrating string: sound and frequency

When a string vibrates, a fixed point on it may move approximately sinusoidally. Its displacement from equilibrium (in millimeters) is modeled by y(t)=2 sin(440πt), with time in seconds.

  1. Identify the amplitude and angular frequency, including units.
  2. Determine the period and frequency in cycles per second.
  3. How many complete oscillations occur in 0.1 seconds?
  4. Now consider y(t)=2 sin(880πt). What physical feature changes, and what stays the same?

3. Recover a model from measurements

A monitoring buoy rises and falls in approximately periodic water motion. Its height is measured relative to a fixed reference point. The recorded maximum height is 3.5 m, the minimum is −0.5 m, and consecutive maximum heights occur 10 seconds apart. At t=0 the buoy crosses its midline going upward.

  1. Calculate the amplitude, midline, and period from these measurements.
  2. Build a sine model for the height h(t). Explain the choice of each parameter.
  3. Use the model to predict the buoy height at t=2.5 seconds and t=7.5 seconds.
  4. Discuss one physical reason real buoy data might not follow your mathematical model exactly.

A model is a useful description of observed behavior, not a claim that every real-world cycle is identical. Always check the assumptions before interpreting a prediction.
