1. An inaccessible ridge

A ridge is 45 m higher than an instrument. Its base is 75 m away horizontally. Find the elevation angle.

A hint to get started

The known ratio is opposite over adjacent. An inverse tangent returns its corresponding principal angle.

Compare your solution after an attempt
\(\tan\theta=45/75=3/5\), so \(\theta\approx31.0^\circ\). Arctangent takes the ratio as input and returns an angle. The reciprocal expression takes a tangent of an input angle first and then reciprocates its value; it does not recover the measured elevation.

2. A robot's heading

A robot points at \(150^\circ\) counterclockwise from east. A program receives only the vertical coordinate \(\sin150^\circ\) and reconstructs an angle with arcsine.

A hint to get started

Compare the two points at \(30^\circ\) and \(150^\circ\): they share a height but lie on opposite sides.

Compare your solution after an attempt
The vertical coordinate is \(1/2\), so arcsine returns \(30^\circ\). The horizontal coordinate is \(-\sqrt3/2\), placing the robot in Quadrant II. Arccosine returns \(150^\circ\), which lies in its principal range \([0^\circ,180^\circ]\).

3. Two camera rotations

A camera platform turns through \(45^\circ\), followed by \(30^\circ\). Its final direction has angle \(75^\circ\).

A hint to get started

Use the exact \(30^\circ\) and \(45^\circ\) values. Keep the minus sign in cosine's formula.

Compare your solution after an attempt
\(\cos75^\circ=(\sqrt6-\sqrt2)/4\), and \(\sin75^\circ=(\sqrt6+\sqrt2)/4\). Their squared sum is \(((8-4\sqrt3)+(8+4\sqrt3))/16=1\), matching a point on the unit circle.
