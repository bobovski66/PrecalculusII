Module 2 Applications

Six functions in measurement, navigation, and geometry

Related interactive demo: Six Trigonometric Functions Explorer. Open the demo to explore the mathematical ideas while working through these applications.

1. Surveying a radio tower

From a point on level ground, a surveyor measures an angle of elevation of 34° to the top of a tower. The horizontal distance from the observer to the base of the tower is 85 m. Treat the tower as vertical and ignore the observer's eye height.

  1. Draw the corresponding right triangle and label the known values.
  2. Decide which trigonometric ratio relates the unknown height to the known horizontal distance, and explain your decision.
  3. Calculate the tower's height to the nearest tenth of a meter.
  4. If the surveyor moves to a point closer to the tower, explain what happens to the angle of elevation.

2. The direction of a displacement

An autonomous ground robot moves 12 m east and then 5 m north. Its displacement is the straight-line vector from its starting location to its final location.

  1. Draw the right triangle and compute the total displacement magnitude.
  2. Find the angle the displacement makes with the eastward direction.
  3. Find exact sine, cosine, and tangent values for that angle.
  4. Explain why specifying only the distance 13 m would not fully describe the displacement.

3. When division fails

A point rotates around the unit circle. Its vertical coordinate is sine and its horizontal coordinate is cosine. A computer computes the tangent using the quotient sin θ / cos θ.

  1. What happens at 90°? Why does the formula fail at exactly this angle?
  2. Compare tangent values at 89° and 91°. Explain the sign change geometrically.
  3. Explain why replacing an undefined tangent value by zero would be mathematically misleading.

Modeling reminder: an undefined output is a feature of the mathematical relationship. Software should communicate that limitation rather than hide it.
