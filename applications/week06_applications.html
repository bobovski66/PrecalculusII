Module 6 Applications

Vectors in navigation, force, and geometry

1. A delivery robot takes a shortcut

A robot follows two perpendicular hallways, traveling 12 m east and then 5 m north. It may instead travel directly between its starting and ending positions.

  1. Write the total displacement as a vector.
  2. Calculate the length and direction of the direct route.
  3. Compare the distance traveled along the hallways with the displacement magnitude.
  4. Explain why a vector captures information that distance alone cannot.

2. Two forces act on a platform

A platform is pushed by two horizontal forces, F₁=⟨25,10⟩ N and F₂=⟨−5,15⟩ N, measured in east and north components.

  1. Find the resultant force vector.
  2. Determine its magnitude and direction relative to east.
  3. Explain the effect of the negative east component in F₂.

3. Work done pulling equipment

A worker pulls a cart with a constant force of magnitude 80 N, acting at 30° above the horizontal. The cart moves 6 m horizontally, without appreciable vertical displacement.

  1. Write the force and displacement as vectors.
  2. Use their dot product to calculate work in joules.
  3. Why does the vertical component of force contribute no work in this model?
  4. How would the work change if the force were parallel to the displacement?

4. A familiar triangle law returns

Two displacement vectors have lengths 7 km and 10 km with an angle of 60° between them. The difference vector connects their endpoints.

  1. Use the dot-product formula to compute the squared magnitude of the difference.
  2. Find its length and compare with the Module 5 Law of Cosines.
  3. Explain how trigonometry and vectors are describing the same geometry in different languages.

Modeling reminder: always indicate units and explain the physical meaning of a vector's components.
