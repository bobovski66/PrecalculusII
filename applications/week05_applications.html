Module 5 Applications

Angles, distances, and multiple solutions

Related interactive demo: Trigonometric Equation Explorer. Open the demo to explore the mathematical ideas while working through these applications.

1. Rotating signal threshold

A rotating sensor reports a normalized vertical component modeled by s(t)=sin(πt/6), where t is measured in seconds. An alarm triggers whenever s(t)=1/2. We want to know every triggering instant in the first 12 seconds, not merely the first.

  1. Solve sin(πt/6)=1/2 for 0≤t<12. List all triggering times.
  2. Explain how the period determines when the pattern repeats.
  3. If the wheel spins twice as fast, how does the number of triggers change over the same 12 seconds?

2. Surveying an inaccessible island

Two observers A and B stand 200 m apart on a straight shoreline. They sight a marker C on an island. The interior angles of triangle ABC are A=47° and B=68°. The distance AC cannot be measured directly.

  1. Find angle C, describing why the triangle's angles must total 180°.
  2. Use the Law of Sines to calculate AC to the nearest meter.
  3. Explain why the Law of Sines is appropriate for the given information.

3. Two roads and a direct route

Two roads begin at the same trail junction. One follows a route for 7 km, the other for 10 km, with an included angle of 60° between their directions. A proposed connector joins their endpoints.

  1. Sketch the triangle and identify the side opposite the 60° angle.
  2. Use the Law of Cosines to calculate the connector's length.
  3. Consider an included angle of 90° instead. What familiar theorem does the Law of Cosines become?

4. When measurements allow two triangles

A surveyor knows angle A=30° and opposite side a=5 m, together with a second side b=8 m. The unknown angle B is found using sin B=0.8. Because sine is positive in two quadrants, there may be two geometric solutions.

  1. Find both values of B in degrees.
  2. For each B, calculate the remaining angle C.
  3. Explain why both triangles satisfy the original measurements.
  4. What additional observation could distinguish the two possibilities?

A model should tell us how many solutions the observations actually support. An inverse trig calculator's principal answer may not be the only geometrically possible answer.
