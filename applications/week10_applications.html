Module 10 Applications

Making connections across Precalculus II

1. A robotic arm revisits its position

The end of a 2-meter robotic arm rotates at a constant 30° per second, starting on the positive horizontal axis. A camera records the endpoint every 3 seconds.

  1. Write its continuous parametric position equations.
  2. Give the equivalent complex expression for the endpoint.
  3. Find the complex multiplier between consecutive camera frames.
  4. How many camera frames are required before the observed positions repeat?

2. A controlled spiral

A simulated particle begins 4 units east of the origin. Every second it is rotated 60° counterclockwise and scaled to 75% of its previous distance from the origin.

  1. Write the complex recurrence and an explicit expression for the nth point.
  2. Calculate the exact position after three updates.
  3. Describe the path formed by the sampled points and the behavior of the radius.
  4. Explain why the angles repeat but the positions do not.

3. Compare a circle and an ellipse

Two particles have motion laws P(t)=(3cos t,3sin t) and Q(t)=(3cos t,2sin t), both for 0≤t≤2π.

  1. Eliminate the parameters and identify both curves.
  2. Compare their dimensions and periods.
  3. Explain why the geometric shape alone does not specify how quickly the point traverses it.

4. Repeated displacements and a total

A robotic platform takes steps represented by Δzₙ=(1/2)ⁿ(1+i), beginning at n=0, from initial position z₀=0.

  1. Calculate the first three displacement vectors.
  2. Find the position after N steps using a geometric sum.
  3. Find the limiting position as N increases without bound.
  4. Explain the distinction between the step sequence and the sequence of positions.

The purpose of a model is to preserve the information needed to answer the question. Different mathematical descriptions highlight different features of one system.
