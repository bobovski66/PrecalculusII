\section{Synthesis: Motion and Patterns}
\noindent\textbf{Central question.} How can the different mathematical languages of this course describe one evolving system?

\subsection{One motion, several descriptions}
Imagine a point traveling counterclockwise around a circle of radius $R$ with angular speed $\omega$. Its angle at time $t$ is $\theta(t)=\theta_0+\omega t$. In parametric form,
\[
x(t)=R\cos(\theta_0+\omega t),\qquad
y(t)=R\sin(\theta_0+\omega t).
\]
Its polar form is $(r,\theta)=(R,\theta_0+\omega t)$, and its complex form is
\[
\boxed{z(t)=R\bigl(\cos(\theta_0+\omega t)+i\sin(\theta_0+\omega t)\bigr)}.
\]
These are not competing models: they encode the same physical position in different coordinate languages.

\subsection{Velocity, vectors, and periodicity}
Over a short time interval $\Delta t$, the displacement vector is
\[
\Delta\mathbf r=\langle x(t+\Delta t)-x(t),y(t+\Delta t)-y(t)\rangle.
\]
For constant angular speed the point completes a revolution after $T=2\pi/|\omega|$ when $\omega\ne0$. Its horizontal and vertical coordinates repeat with that period. The direction of motion is tangent to the circle and perpendicular to the radius: this is the geometric heart of circular motion.

\subsection{Sampling creates a sequence}
Suppose we record the position every $\Delta t$ seconds. The resulting sequence of complex positions is
\[
z_n=R\bigl(\cos(\theta_0+n\delta)+i\sin(\theta_0+n\delta)\bigr),
\qquad \delta=\omega\Delta t.
\]
Writing $u=\cos\delta+i\sin\delta$ gives
\[
\boxed{z_{n+1}=uz_n,\qquad z_n=u^nz_0.}
\]
De Moivre's theorem then explains every sample as a repeated rotation. If $\delta=2\pi/m$ for a positive integer $m$, the sampled orbit closes after $m$ steps (provided this is the smallest such positive step count). If $\delta/(2\pi)$ is irrational, no two different samples have exactly the same position.

\subsection{Scaling and rotation together}
Now consider the recurrence $z_{n+1}=qz_n$ where
\[
q=\rho(\cos\delta+i\sin\delta).
\]
Its explicit solution is $z_n=q^nz_0$. Consequently,
\[
|z_n|=\rho^n|z_0|,\qquad \arg z_n\equiv\arg z_0+n\delta\pmod{2\pi}
\]
for nonzero terms. If $0<\rho<1$, the points spiral toward the origin; if $\rho>1$, they spiral outward; and if $\rho=1$, they remain on a circle. Thus a geometric sequence and a rotation combine to produce a spiral.

\subsection{Sums represent accumulated effects}
If a system moves by step vectors $\mathbf v_0,\mathbf v_1,\ldots$, its total displacement after $N$ steps is the vector sum $\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbf v_n$. For a complex-valued geometric pattern $\Delta z_n=aq^n$ with $q\ne1$,
\[
z_N-z_0=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}aq^n=a\frac{1-q^N}{1-q}.
\]
Notice that the location sequence $z_n$ and the step sequence $\Delta z_n$ are different objects. Careful modeling starts by distinguishing what is being measured.

\subsection{Different curves arise from different constraints}
Circular and elliptical motion can be expressed parametrically. A projectile follows a parabola when horizontal velocity is constant and vertical acceleration is constant. Polar formulas express rays and radial patterns directly. The useful representation depends on whether we need a shape, a position, a direction, or a rule for change.

\subsection{A complete example}
A sensor begins at $z_0=2$ in the complex plane. Every second its controller multiplies the position by $q=\tfrac12(\cos60^\circ+i\sin60^\circ)$. After $n$ steps,
\[
z_n=2\left(\frac12\right)^n
\bigl(\cos(60^\circ n)+i\sin(60^\circ n)\bigr).
\]
After three steps the modulus is $1/4$, and the direction is $180^\circ$. Hence $z_3=-1/4$. The modulus obeys a geometric sequence, the direction obeys an arithmetic progression of angles, and the position follows a discrete inward spiral.

\subsection{Exercises}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Write Cartesian, polar, and complex formulas for a circle of radius 5 rotating once every 20 seconds, starting on the positive horizontal axis.
\item Sample that motion every five seconds. Give its first five complex positions and identify its repetition period in samples.
\item For $z_{n+1}=iz_n$ and $z_0=1+i$, calculate $z_1,\ldots,z_4$ and describe the geometry.
\item For $z_{n+1}=(1/2)iz_n$, $z_0=4$, find $z_4$ and explain the change in magnitude and argument.
\item A point travels along $x=3\cos t$, $y=2\sin t$. Eliminate the parameter and describe one complete traversal.
\item Explain why a constant-speed circular trajectory is periodic but the spiral $z_n=(0.8e^{i\pi/3})^nz_0$ is not periodic when $z_0\ne0$.
\item Compute the finite sum $1+i+i^2+i^3$ and interpret its geometric cancellation.
\item Describe a real-world process that could be analyzed with at least three of the course's mathematical representations.
\end{enumerate}
