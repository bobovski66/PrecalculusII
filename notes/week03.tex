\section{Periodic Functions and Their Graphs}
\noindent Periodic functions describe quantities that repeat as their input increases. Trigonometric functions provide fundamental examples: their periodicity arises from rotation, and their graphs can be transformed to represent a wide range of oscillatory processes.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Learning objectives.} Identify periods, amplitudes, midlines, and phase shifts; construct transformed sine and cosine graphs; distinguish angular frequency from frequency; and determine sinusoidal models from geometric or measured information.

\subsection{Circular motion and periodicity}
For a point traveling around the unit circle with angle $\theta$, its vertical coordinate is $y=\sin\theta$ and its horizontal coordinate is $x=\cos\theta$. Each revolution restores the same coordinates. Hence
\[
\sin(\theta+2\pi)=\sin\theta,\qquad \cos(\theta+2\pi)=\cos\theta.
\]
A function $f$ is \emph{periodic} if there exists $T>0$ such that $f(x+T)=f(x)$ for every $x$ in its domain. When a least positive such $T$ exists, it is the \emph{fundamental period}. The fundamental periods of sine and cosine are both $2\pi$. Moreover, $\sin0=0$ and $\cos0=1$.

\subsection{Vertical transformations}
Consider $y=A\sin x+D$. Its midline is $y=D$, its amplitude is $|A|$, and its range is $[D-|A|,D+|A|]$. A negative $A$ reflects the wave across the midline.
\begin{example}The function $y=3\sin x-2$ has amplitude $3$, midline $y=-2$, maximum $1$, and minimum $-5$.\end{example}


For a sinusoid with maximum $M$ and minimum $m$, the amplitude and midline are
\[
|A|=\frac{M-m}{2},
\qquad D=\frac{M+m}{2}.
\]
These relations determine the vertical parameters without knowing the phase or period.

\begin{example}
A periodically varying temperature has maximum $26^\circ$C and minimum
$14^\circ$C. Its midline is $20^\circ$C and its amplitude is $6^\circ$C.
These values do not determine the period or phase.
\end{example}

\begin{practice}
A sinusoidal signal ranges from $-7$ to $5$. Determine its amplitude,
midline, and range, and sketch one possible cycle.
\end{practice}

\subsection{Period and horizontal scaling}
For $y=\sin(Bx)$ with $B\neq0$, one full cycle occurs when $Bx$ changes by $2\pi$; the period is $T=2\pi/|B|$.
\begin{example}The graph of $y=\cos(4x)$ repeats every $\pi/2$ units of input.\end{example}
When $x$ denotes time measured in seconds, the quantity $|B|$ is angular frequency in radians per second, and ordinary frequency is $f=1/T=|B|/(2\pi)$ cycles per second.


A phase change of $2\pi$ radians corresponds to one cycle. If the
argument of a sinusoidal function is $\omega t+\phi$, then its period is
$T=2\pi/|\omega|$, and its frequency is
\[
f=\frac1T=\frac{|\omega|}{2\pi}.
\]
Frequency is measured in cycles per unit time, while angular frequency
is measured in radians per unit time. These units describe the same
repetition rate in different ways.

\begin{example}
A mechanism rotates at $90$ revolutions per minute. Its frequency is
$1.5$ cycles per second, and its angular speed is
$2\pi(1.5)=3\pi$ radians per second. Its period is $2/3$ second.
\end{example}

\subsection{Phase shift}
A useful general form is
\[
\boxed{y=A\sin(B(x-C))+D}.
\]
Here $C$ is the horizontal shift, $D$ the midline, $|A|$ the amplitude, and $2\pi/|B|$ the period. We should distinguish $B(x-C)$ from $Bx-C$: factoring first prevents an incorrect phase shift.
\begin{example}For $y=2\sin(3x-\pi)+1=2\sin(3(x-\pi/3))+1$, the period is $2\pi/3$, the shift is $\pi/3$ to the right, the amplitude is $2$, and the midline is $1$.\end{example}


The parameterization assumes $B\ne0$. In factored form, the phase
shift is $C$. In the equivalent form $A\sin(Bx+\phi)+D$, the same shift
is $C=-\phi/B$. The sign of $B$ may be taken positive in a standard
sine model by modifying the sign of $A$.

\subsection{Five landmark points}
Rather than sketching a sinusoid by guesswork, divide one period into four equal intervals. For $A>0$, sine begins on the midline, rises to a maximum after one quarter-period, returns to the midline, reaches a minimum, then returns to the midline. A negative amplitude reverses the rises and falls.

\begin{practice}Find five landmark points of $y=-2\cos(\pi(x-1))+3$, showing one entire period.\end{practice}


For $y=A\sin(B(x-C))+D$ with $B>0$, write
$T=2\pi/B$. One cycle is determined by the following five points:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
x&C&C+T/4&C+T/2&C+3T/4&C+T\\ \hline
y&D&D+A&D&D-A&D
\end{array}
\]
For cosine, the corresponding outputs are $D+A,D,D-A,D,D+A$.
These points determine the positions of maxima, minima, and midline
crossings; the smooth sinusoidal curve passes through them.

\begin{example}
For $y=-2\cos(\pi(x-1))+3$, the period is $T=2$.
Beginning at $x=1$, the five landmark points are
\[
(1,1),\quad (3/2,3),\quad (2,5),\quad (5/2,3),\quad (3,1).
\]
The negative coefficient places the minimum at the start of the cycle.
\end{example}

\subsection{Models of repeating motion}
A Ferris wheel has radius $9$ m, its center is $11$ m above ground, and it makes one revolution every $30$ s. If a passenger begins at the rightmost position and travels counterclockwise, their height is
\[
h(t)=11+9\sin\left(\frac{2\pi}{30}t\right).
\]
Its amplitude is $9$ m, midline is $11$ m, and period is $30$ s. A sinusoidal model can describe a wheel, a spring, a tide, or a musical tone, provided its parameters actually correspond to the chosen physical quantities.


\subsection{Constructing a model from measurements}
Suppose a buoy reaches a maximum height of $5$ meters and a minimum
height of $1$ meter. Successive maxima are separated by $8$ seconds.
Taking $t=0$ at a maximum yields the model
\[
h(t)=3+2\cos\left(\frac{\pi t}{4}\right).
\]
The midline is $3$ meters, the amplitude is $2$ meters, and the
angular frequency is $\pi/4$ radians per second. If the time origin
were chosen at an upward midline crossing, a sine model would be more
convenient. The physical motion is unchanged by this choice of
representation.

\begin{practice}
A tide measurement varies between $0.8$ m and $3.6$ m and repeats
every $12$ hours. Write a cosine model assuming that the maximum occurs
at $t=2$ hours. State the meaning and units of every parameter.
\end{practice}

\subsection{Tangent and reciprocal graphs}
The tangent function repeats after $\pi$, not $2\pi$, because both sine and cosine change sign after a half turn and their ratio remains the same:
\[
\tan(x+\pi)=\tan x.
\]
Tangent has vertical asymptotes at $x=\pi/2+k\pi$. The secant and cosecant graphs arise as reciprocals of cosine and sine; their breaks occur wherever the respective denominator is zero.


Because $\tan x=\sin x/\cos x$, its zeros occur at $x=k\pi$ and
its vertical asymptotes at $x=\pi/2+k\pi$. Similarly,
$\cot x=\cos x/\sin x$ is undefined at integer multiples of $\pi$.
The graphs of secant and cosecant lie outside the strip $-1<y<1$
because their absolute values are at least $1$ where defined.

\subsection{Exercises}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Find the amplitude, midline, period, and range of $y=-4\sin(2x)+1$.
\item Rewrite $y=3\cos(5x-\pi)+2$ in shifted form and identify the phase shift.
\item Give five landmark points for $y=2\sin(x-\pi/2)$.
\item Explain why $y=\sin(2x)$ completes two periods on $[0,2\pi]$.
\item A wheel of radius $6$ m rotates once every $24$ seconds. Its center is $8$ m above the ground. Model the rider's height when starting at the rightmost point.
\item Identify the vertical asymptotes of $y=\tan(2x)$ on $[0,2\pi)$.
\item Sketch one period of $y=2\sec x$ and indicate all discontinuities.
\item Explain how you can determine amplitude and midline directly from observed maximum and minimum values.
\item Determine a sinusoidal model with maximum $8$, minimum $-2$, period $10$, and maximum at $x=3$.
\item Explain why $A\sin(B(x-C))+D$ is constant when $A=0$, and why the usual fundamental-period formula does not apply.
\item A motor oscillates at $2.5$ cycles per second. Determine its angular frequency and period.
\item Give the domain of $y=\tan(3x)$ and identify two consecutive vertical asymptotes.
\item Determine the amplitude and midline from a sinusoid with maximum $12.4$ and minimum $7.6$.
\end{enumerate}
