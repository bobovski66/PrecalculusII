1. Principal values

Use the Inverse Trigonometry Explorer at \(150^\circ\).

A hint to get started

Arcsine uses \([-90^\circ,90^\circ]\), while arccosine uses \([0^\circ,180^\circ]\).

Compare your solution after an attempt
Arcsine returns \(30^\circ\), since the sine is \(1/2\). Arccosine returns \(150^\circ\), since that original angle is in its principal range.

2. Function versus reciprocal

Let \(\theta=30^\circ\).

A hint to get started

An inverse function reverses an input-output relationship. A reciprocal divides 1 by a value.

Compare your solution after an attempt
The values are \(30^\circ,2,2\). Arcsine returns an angle; cosecant and the reciprocal return the same number.

3. Restricted branches

Explore \(-120^\circ,-30^\circ,30^\circ,120^\circ\).

A hint to get started

First find the sine, then choose the matching angle inside the principal range.

Compare your solution after an attempt
The outputs are \(-60^\circ,-30^\circ,30^\circ,60^\circ\). Only the original angles inside \([-90^\circ,90^\circ]\) return unchanged.

4. Recover an angle from a ratio

A ladder reaches 12 ft up a wall, with its foot 5 ft from the wall. The wall and ground meet at a right angle.

A hint to get started

Use \(\tan\theta=12/5\).

Compare your solution after an attempt
\(\theta=\arctan(12/5)\approx67.4^\circ\). Both legs are known, so tangent connects them directly.

5. Prove an identity

Start with \(\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1\).

A hint to get started

\(\sin^2x/\cos^2x=\tan^2x\), and \(1/\cos^2x=\sec^2x\).

Compare your solution after an attempt
The result is \(1+\tan^2x=\sec^2x\), valid when \(\cos x\ne0\), so \(x\ne\pi/2+k\pi\) for integers \(k\).

6. Check a conjecture

A classmate proposes \(\sin(a+b)=\sin a+\sin b\) for all angles.

A hint to get started

One example where the sides differ disproves a claim that they agree for every input.

Compare your solution after an attempt
The left side is \(\sin60^\circ=\sqrt3/2\), while the right side is 1. The unequal values disprove the proposed identity.

7. Double-angle connection

Use \(\sin(a+b)=\sin a\cos b+\cos a\sin b\).

A hint to get started

The two terms on the right become equal.

Compare your solution after an attempt
\(\sin(2x)=2\sin x\cos x\), so \(\sin60^\circ=2(1/2)(\sqrt3/2)=\sqrt3/2\).
