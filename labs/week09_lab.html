1. Repeated addition

In the Sequence Explorer, choose arithmetic with \(a_1=4,d=3\).

A hint to get started

From term 1 to term 20 there are 19 additions.

Compare your solution after an attempt
The terms are \(4,7,10,13,16,19\). The formula is \(a_n=4+3(n-1)\), giving \(a_{20}=61\).

2. The accumulated total

Keep \(a_1=4,d=3\).

A hint to get started

Use \(S_n=n(a_1+a_n)/2\).

Compare your solution after an attempt
The tenth term is 31, while \(S_{10}=10(4+31)/2=175\). The latter includes all ten terms.

3. Repeated multiplication

Choose geometric with \(a_1=80,q=0.5\).

A hint to get started

Every term is half the preceding term.

Compare your solution after an attempt
The terms are \(80,40,20,10,5,2.5\), with \(a_n=80(1/2)^{n-1}\). The positive values keep halving.

4. A bounded running total

Use \(a_1=5,q=0.6\).

A hint to get started

Use \(S_n=5(1-0.6^n)/(1-0.6)\).

Compare your solution after an attempt
\(S_5=11.528,\ S_{10}\approx12.4244,\ S_{20}\approx12.4995\). The limit is 12.5, because \(0.6^n\) approaches zero.

5. Growth model

A population starts at 200 organisms and grows 10% per generation. Label the starting observation \(P_0\).

A hint to get started

A 10% increase multiplies by 1.10.

Compare your solution after an attempt
\(P_0=200,\ P_{n+1}=1.1P_n,\ P_n=200(1.1)^n\). The first values are 200,220,242, and \(P_5=322.102\). The model may give noninteger estimates.

6. Feedback toward an equilibrium

Start with \(a_1=0\) and use \(a_{n+1}=(a_n+8)/2\). Work this part by calculation.

A hint to get started

Each new value lies halfway between the preceding value and 8.

Compare your solution after an attempt
The terms are \(0,4,6,7,7.5,7.75\), and the equilibrium is 8. The gaps are \(8,4,2,1,0.5,0.25\), with ratio \(1/2\).

7. Interpret a model

Compare a theater adding a fixed number of seats per row with an account increasing by a fixed percentage each year.

A hint to get started

A percentage acts on the current amount; a fixed addition is the same number each time.

Compare your solution after an attempt
Fixed seat increases are arithmetic; fixed percentage growth is geometric. One row's seat count is a term; seats in the first several rows form a partial sum.
