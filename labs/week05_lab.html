1. Multiple sine solutions

Use the Equation Explorer with sine, \(B=1\), and target 0.5.

A hint to get started

The points have the same positive height on opposite sides of the vertical axis.

Compare your solution after an attempt
The solutions are \(\pi/6,5\pi/6\). Arcsine chooses \(\pi/6\) from its principal range.

2. Frequency changes solutions

Keep the sine target at 0.5 and compare \(B=2\) with \(B=3\).

A hint to get started

Before dividing by \(B\), include every full turn in the interval for the inside angle.

Compare your solution after an attempt
For \(B=2\): \(x=\pi/12,5\pi/12,13\pi/12,17\pi/12\). For \(B=3\): \(x=\pi/18,5\pi/18,13\pi/18,17\pi/18,25\pi/18,29\pi/18\).

3. Factoring before dividing

Solve \(2\sin^2x-\sin x=0\) on \([0,2\pi)\).

A hint to get started

Treat sine as one variable while you factor, then return to the angle.

Compare your solution after an attempt
\(\sin x(2\sin x-1)=0\). The first branch gives \(0,\pi\); the second gives \(\pi/6,5\pi/6\). Dividing by sine would lose \(0,\pi\).

4. A triangle from two angles

A triangle has \(A=40^\circ,B=65^\circ\), and opposite side \(a=9\) cm.

A hint to get started

Pair \(9\) with \(\sin40^\circ\), and \(b\) with \(\sin65^\circ\).

Compare your solution after an attempt
\(C=75^\circ\), and \(b=9\sin65^\circ/\sin40^\circ\approx12.69\) cm. The larger angle has the longer opposite side.

5. The ambiguous case

Suppose \(A=30^\circ,a=5,b=8\). There may be two triangles.

A hint to get started

The supplementary angle has the same sine. Each candidate must also satisfy \(A+B<180^\circ\).

Compare your solution after an attempt
\(\sin B=0.8\). Thus \(B_1\approx53.13^\circ,B_2\approx126.87^\circ\), giving \(C_1\approx96.87^\circ,C_2\approx23.13^\circ\). Both triangles fit.

6. Cosine law and geometry

Two sides measure 7 m and 10 m, with included angle \(60^\circ\).

A hint to get started

The unknown side is opposite the included angle.

Compare your solution after an attempt
\(c^2=49+100-140\cos60^\circ=79\), so \(c=\sqrt{79}\approx8.89\) m. At \(90^\circ\), the cosine term is zero.

7. Choose a method

Decide how to begin each problem: \(\sin x=0.3\) on one turn; a triangle with two angles and one side; a triangle with two sides and the included angle.

A hint to get started

Match the method to the known information before calculating.

Compare your solution after an attempt
Use the unit circle and inverse sine for the equation; the Law of Sines for a complete opposite pair; the Law of Cosines for two sides with the included angle. Check intervals, angle sums, opposite side order, or the original equation.
