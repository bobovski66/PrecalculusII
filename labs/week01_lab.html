Lab: Rotation into Coordinates

ReadingUnit circleCircle to wavePracticeLabApplicationsQuiz

Investigate how turning becomes coordinates. Use the Unit Circle Explorer and Circle to Wave. Work through all seven questions; use observations to explain your reasoning.

Your responses are saved on this device as you type. Prepare a submission copy to upload to Canvas, or print a PDF.

1. Observe a turn

Open the unit-circle demo. Set the angle to 30°, 90°, 180°, and 270°. Record the coordinates at each angle and describe how each pair corresponds to the point’s position.

Use a sketch to check the signs. Record both the angle and the ordered pair; coordinates are not angle measures.

2. Radians from arc length

A circle has radius 8 cm and a marked arc 10 cm long. Calculate its angle in radians, then convert that angle to degrees (nearest tenth). Show the steps and units.

Begin with \(\theta=s/r\). Convert the result using \(180^\circ/\pi\). Keep the exact ratio until the final rounding.

3. Multiple turns

Set the explorer to 45°, then 405° and −315°. What do you notice about their coordinates? Explain using full revolutions.

Compare each angle with 45° using additions or subtractions of 360°. Distinguish final position from total rotation.

4. Does radius change sine?

Compare 60° with radii 1 and 3 in the explorer. What changes, and what remains fixed? Explain why sine and cosine can be defined using the unit circle.

The explorer displays physical coordinates and unit-circle coordinates separately. Explain which quantities scale with radius.

5. From circle to wave

Open Circle to Wave. Identify the vertical coordinate at angles 0°, 90°, 180°, 270°, and 360°. How do these values determine the sine graph?

Follow the green coordinate. On the graph, angle is the input and vertical coordinate is the output.

6. Modeling a wheel

A Ferris wheel has radius 15 m, and a passenger starts at the rightmost point of the wheel. After a counterclockwise rotation of 2π/3 radians, what is the passenger’s vertical displacement relative to the center? Give an exact answer and an approximate value.

Find the unit-circle vertical coordinate first, then scale by the radius. The question asks for displacement from the center, not height above ground.

7. Reflect

Explain to a student who missed class why 180° equals π radians. Describe the geometric relationship between angle, radius, and arc length.

Connect a half-circumference with the radius. Explain the ratio in words as well as symbols.
