1. Observe a turn

Use the Unit Circle Explorer with radius 1.

A hint to get started

Read horizontal position first and vertical position second.

Compare your solution after an attempt
At \(30^\circ\), \((x,y)=(\sqrt3/2,1/2)\); at \(90^\circ\), \((0,1)\); at \(180^\circ\), \((-1,0)\); at \(270^\circ\), \((0,-1)\). Cosine is horizontal; sine is vertical.

2. Radians from arc length

A circle has radius 8 cm and a marked arc of length 10 cm.

A hint to get started

One radian corresponds to an arc equal to the radius. Multiply radians by \(180^\circ/\pi\) to convert.

Compare your solution after an attempt
\(\theta=10/8=5/4=1.25\) radians. In degrees, \((5/4)(180/\pi)\approx71.6^\circ\).

3. Multiple turns

In the unit-circle explorer, compare \(45^\circ,\ 405^\circ,\ -315^\circ\).

A hint to get started

A full turn changes the angle by \(360^\circ\) and returns the point to its starting location.

Compare your solution after an attempt
\(405^\circ=45^\circ+360^\circ\), and \(-315^\circ=45^\circ-360^\circ\). All give \((\sqrt2/2,\sqrt2/2)\). Their total rotations differ by full turns.

4. Does radius change sine?

Compare a \(60^\circ\) turn at radii 1 and 3 in the unit-circle explorer.

A hint to get started

Divide each physical coordinate by the radius to return to the unit circle.

Compare your solution after an attempt
The coordinates are \((1/2,\sqrt3/2)\) and \((3/2,3\sqrt3/2)\). Both coordinates triple, while \(x/r=1/2\) and \(y/r=\sqrt3/2\) stay fixed.

5. From circle to wave

Open Circle to Wave.

A hint to get started

Follow the vertical coordinate, then locate the same angle on the graph.

Compare your solution after an attempt
The values are \(0,1,0,-1,0\). The graph rises from zero to 1, returns to zero, falls to -1, and returns to zero. Angle is the input; vertical coordinate is the output.

6. Modeling a wheel

A wheel has radius 15 m. A passenger begins at the rightmost point and turns counterclockwise through \(2\pi/3\).

A hint to get started

The angle is \(120^\circ\). Multiply its sine by the radius.

Compare your solution after an attempt
\(\sin(2\pi/3)=\sqrt3/2\). Displacement is \(15\sqrt3/2\approx12.99\) m above the center.

7. Explain radian measure

Imagine explaining a half-turn to a student who missed class.

A hint to get started

The full circumference is \(2\pi r\).

Compare your solution after an attempt
A half-circle has arc length \(\pi r\), so the angle is \(\pi r/r=\pi\) radians. Radian measure counts how many radius lengths fit along the arc.
