2. Different labels, same point

Begin with the polar point \((2,60^\circ)\).

A hint to get started

For negative radius, add a half-turn to the angle and reverse the radius sign.

Compare your solution after an attempt
Examples are \((2,420^\circ)\) and \((-2,240^\circ)\). Each represents \((1,\sqrt3)\).

3. A number on the complex plane

Let \(z=-\sqrt3+i\).

A hint to get started

The coordinate signs place it in Quadrant II.

Compare your solution after an attempt
The modulus is 2 and an argument is \(150^\circ\). Thus \(z=2(\cos150^\circ+i\sin150^\circ)\).

4. Complex addition versus multiplication

Let \(z=1+i,\ w=1-i\).

A hint to get started

The arguments are \(45^\circ\) and \(-45^\circ\), and both moduli are \(\sqrt2\).

Compare your solution after an attempt
Both results are 2. Addition cancels the imaginary components. Multiplication gives modulus 2 and angle zero.

5. Rotating by i

In the Polar and Complex Explorer, set the second modulus to 1 and its angle to \(90^\circ\).

A hint to get started

Multiplication by \(i\) sends \((a,b)\) to \((-b,a)\).

Compare your solution after an attempt
The length stays fixed and the angle increases by \(90^\circ\). The product is \(-1+2i\).

6. Scaling and turning

Set \(z\) to modulus 2 and angle \(30^\circ\), and \(w\) to modulus 3 and angle \(60^\circ\).

A hint to get started

Multiply moduli and add arguments.

Compare your solution after an attempt
The product has modulus 6, argument \(90^\circ\), and rectangular form \(6i\).
