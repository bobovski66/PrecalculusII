1. Build the six functions

Open the Six-Function Explorer at \(60^\circ\).

A hint to get started

Start with \((\cos60^\circ,\sin60^\circ)=(1/2,\sqrt3/2)\).

Compare your solution after an attempt
\(\sin=\sqrt3/2,\cos=1/2,\tan=\sqrt3,\cot=\sqrt3/3,\sec=2,\csc=2\sqrt3/3\).

2. Signs and quadrants

Explore \(120^\circ,210^\circ,315^\circ\).

A hint to get started

A reciprocal keeps the sign of its denominator. A quotient is positive when its numerator and denominator have the same sign.

Compare your solution after an attempt
At \(120^\circ\), sine and cosecant are positive. At \(210^\circ\), tangent and cotangent are positive. At \(315^\circ\), cosine and secant are positive.

3. Undefined is not zero

Use the explorer at \(90^\circ\) and \(180^\circ\).

A hint to get started

Use \(\tan=y/x,\cot=x/y,\sec=1/x,\csc=1/y\).

Compare your solution after an attempt
At \(90^\circ\), tangent and secant are undefined; cosine and cotangent are zero. At \(180^\circ\), cotangent and cosecant are undefined; sine and tangent are zero.

4. Reference angles

Find the six exact values at \(5\pi/4\).

A hint to get started

\(5\pi/4=\pi+\pi/4\), so both coordinates are negative.

Compare your solution after an attempt
The point is \((-\sqrt2/2,-\sqrt2/2)\). Tangent and cotangent are 1; secant and cosecant are \(-\sqrt2\).

5. Right-triangle modeling

A right triangle has legs 5 and 12. Choose the acute angle opposite the side of length 5.

A hint to get started

Use the Pythagorean theorem before forming ratios.

Compare your solution after an attempt
The hypotenuse is 13. The ratios are \(\sin=5/13,\cos=12/13,\tan=5/12,\csc=13/5,\sec=13/12,\cot=12/5\).

6. Measuring a height

An angle of elevation to a tree is \(38^\circ\), measured from an instrument at ground level 30 m from its base on level ground.

A hint to get started

The known distance is adjacent to the angle; the height is opposite.

Compare your solution after an attempt
\(\tan38^\circ=h/30\), so \(h=30\tan38^\circ\approx23.4\) m.

7. Explain a large tangent

Open the Reciprocal Graphs Explorer and compare it with the unit-circle picture.

A hint to get started

A numerator near 1 divided by a small positive denominator produces a large positive quotient.

Compare your solution after an attempt
Sine approaches 1 while cosine approaches 0 from above. Thus tangent grows without bound. Tangent itself is undefined at \(90^\circ\).
