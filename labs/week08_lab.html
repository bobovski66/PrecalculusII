1. Parameter versus curve

Open the Curves and Conics Explorer. Its parameter slider gives a fraction of the displayed path.

A hint to get started

The point moves along a fixed curve; the scale controls change the curve itself.

Compare your solution after an attempt
For the circle, the four positions are right, top, left, bottom. The ellipse follows the same order, with different horizontal and vertical scales.

2. Eliminate the parameter

Start with \(x=4\cos t,\ y=3\sin t\).

A hint to get started

Use \(\cos^2t+\sin^2t=1\).

Compare your solution after an attempt
The curve is \(x^2/16+y^2/9=1\), an ellipse. The interval traces its upper half from \((4,0)\) to \((-4,0)\).

3. One curve, different motion

Compare \((\cos t,\sin t)\) with \((\cos2t,\sin2t)\), for \(0\le t\le2\pi\).

A hint to get started

The inside angle advances twice as fast in the second pair.

Compare your solution after an attempt
The positions are \((0,1)\) and \((-1,0)\). Both trace the unit circle counterclockwise; the first makes one turn, the second two.

4. A projectile

A ball launches from the origin at 20 m/s and \(30^\circ\). Use \(g=9.8\text{ m/s}^2\) and ignore air resistance.

A hint to get started

The vertical equation includes the downward term \(-gt^2/2\).

Compare your solution after an attempt
The equations are \(x=10\sqrt3t,\ y=10t-4.9t^2\). After 1 s, the position is \((10\sqrt3,5.1)\) m.

5. Ellipse and its foci

Consider \(x^2/25+y^2/9=1\).

A hint to get started

The larger denominator belongs to the major axis.

Compare your solution after an attempt
The center is \((0,0)\), semiaxes 5 and 3, horizontal major axis, and foci \((\pm4,0)\). The distance sum is 10.

6. Focus and directrix

Consider \(x^2=8y\).

A hint to get started

Distance to a horizontal line is a vertical distance.

Compare your solution after an attempt
Here \(p=2\); vertex \((0,0)\), focus \((0,2)\), directrix \(y=-2\). At \((4,2)\), both distances are 4.

7. Hyperbola

Consider \(x^2/9-y^2/16=1\).

A hint to get started

For a hyperbola, \(c^2=a^2+b^2\). The positive squared term identifies the opening direction.

Compare your solution after an attempt
\(a=3,b=4,c=5\). Vertices \((\pm3,0)\), foci \((\pm5,0)\), asymptotes \(y=\pm4x/3\); branches open left and right.
