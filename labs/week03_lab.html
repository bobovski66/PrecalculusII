1. Identify the transformations

In the Sinusoid Explorer, use \(A=3,B=2,C=1,D=-2\). Its formula is \(y=A\sin(B(x-C))+D\).

A hint to get started

The sine values range from -1 to 1 before scaling and shifting.

Compare your solution after an attempt
The equation is \(y=3\sin(2(x-1))-2\). Amplitude 3, period \(\pi\), shift 1 right, midline -2, minimum -5, maximum 1, range \([-5,1]\).

2. Investigate amplitude

Keep \(B=1,C=0,D=0\). Compare \(A=1,4,-4\).

A hint to get started

Amplitude measures a nonnegative distance from the midline.

Compare your solution after an attempt
The amplitudes are 1,4,4. The last two graphs have range \([-4,4]\) and period \(2\pi\). A negative coefficient reflects the wave across the midline.

3. Investigate period

With \(A=1,C=D=0\), compare \(B=1,2,4\).

A hint to get started

A larger multiplier makes the inside angle complete a turn sooner.

Compare your solution after an attempt
The periods are \(2\pi,\pi,\pi/2\), with 1,2,4 cycles on the interval.

4. Understand phase shifts

Compare \(y=\sin(2(x-1))\) and \(y=\sin(2x-1)\).

A hint to get started

Factor \(2x-1=2(x-1/2)\).

Compare your solution after an attempt
The shifts are 1 and \(1/2\) to the right. Those are also their first nonnegative upward midline crossings.

5. Model a Ferris wheel

A wheel has radius 8 m, center 10 m above ground, and period 40 s. A passenger starts at the rightmost point and moves counterclockwise.

A hint to get started

At the starting point, height is the midline and initially increases.

Compare your solution after an attempt
\(h(t)=10+8\sin(\pi t/20)\). The maximum is 18 m, minimum 2 m; at 10 s the passenger reaches the top, so \(h(10)=18\) m.

6. Recover a model from features

A signal has maximum 7, minimum -1, and period 6 s. At \(t=0\), it crosses its midline upward.

A hint to get started

Average the extremes for the midline; take half their difference for amplitude.

Compare your solution after an attempt
The midline is 3, amplitude 4, and angular frequency \(\pi/3\). A model is \(s(t)=3+4\sin(\pi t/3)\).

7. Connect rotation to a wave
