1. A directed displacement

A walker travels 6 km east, then 8 km north.

A hint to get started

The displacement joins the starting location directly to the final location.

Compare your solution after an attempt
The displacement is \(\langle6,8\rangle\) km with magnitude 10 km. The route length is 14 km.

2. Adding two movements

Use \(\mathbf u=\langle3,4\rangle\) and \(\mathbf v=\langle-2,1\rangle\) in the Vector Explorer.

A hint to get started

Translate the second arrow so it begins at the end of the first.

Compare your solution after an attempt
The sum is \(\langle1,5\rangle\). Following both movements ends one unit right and five units up.

3. Magnitude and scaling

Begin with \(\mathbf u=\langle3,4\rangle\). Compare it with \(2\mathbf u\) and \(-\mathbf u\) in the explorer.

A hint to get started

Multiply each component by the same scalar.

Compare your solution after an attempt
The vectors are \(\langle3,4\rangle,\langle6,8\rangle,\langle-3,-4\rangle\), with lengths 5,10,5. Doubling preserves direction; negation reverses it.

4. Direction from an angle

A 20 N force acts at \(150^\circ\) counterclockwise from the positive horizontal direction.

A hint to get started

Use \(\langle r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta\rangle\).

Compare your solution after an attempt
\(\mathbf F=\langle-10\sqrt3,10\rangle\) N. The squared magnitude is \(300+100=400\).

5. Investigating perpendicularity

Set \(\mathbf u=\langle3,4\rangle,\mathbf v=\langle4,-3\rangle\).

A hint to get started

Both vectors are nonzero, so their lengths are positive.

Compare your solution after an attempt
The dot product is \(3(4)+4(-3)=0\). Thus \(\cos\phi=0\), giving a right angle.

6. The work of a force

A constant force \(\mathbf F=\langle12,5\rangle\) N acts through displacement \(\mathbf d=\langle3,0\rangle\) m.

A hint to get started

Match each force component to the displacement in that same direction.

Compare your solution after an attempt
The work is \(12(3)+5(0)=36\) J. There is no vertical displacement, so the vertical force component contributes zero work.

7. Return to triangle geometry

Let \(\mathbf u=\langle4,0\rangle,\mathbf v=\langle2,2\sqrt3\rangle\).

A hint to get started

The two arrows and the segment joining their endpoints form a triangle.

Compare your solution after an attempt
Both lengths are 4 and the dot product is 8, so \(\cos\phi=1/2\), giving \(60^\circ\). The difference has squared length \(4+12=16\), also \(16+16-32(1/2)=16\).
